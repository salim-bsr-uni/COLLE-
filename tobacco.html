Health Effects of Tobacco

Overview

Tobacco use has substantial harmful effects on human health, primary among them being an increased risk of cardiovascular disease, respiratory disorders, and various forms of cancer.

Cardiovascular Effects

Inhalation of tobacco smoke introduces nicotine, carbon monoxide, and thousands of other harmful chemical compounds into the bloodstream. Nicotine acts as a vasoconstrictor, elevating heart rate and blood pressure, while carbon monoxide reduces the oxygen-carrying capacity of hemoglobin. Over time, this contributes directly to arterial stiffening, atherosclerosis, hypertension, and a heightened risk of myocardial infarction (heart attack) and stroke.

Respiratory Diseases

Smoking causes widespread inflammation and structural damage within the pulmonary airways and alveoli. It is the leading cause of Chronic Obstructive Pulmonary Disease (COPD), which includes chronic bronchitis and emphysema. Chronic exposure to tobacco smoke impairs lung function, reduces immune defenses in the respiratory tract, and leads to persistent, debilitating respiratory symptoms.

Carcinogenic Impact

Tobacco smoke contains at least 70 known carcinogens. DNA damage caused by these toxic substances can trigger uncontrolled cell growth and tumor formation. While lung cancer is the most widely recognized malignancy associated with tobacco, smoking also increases the risk of cancers of the larynx, mouth, esophagus, bladder, pancreas, and stomach.

Secondhand Smoke

Passive exposure to environmental tobacco smoke carries serious health risks for non-smokers. Secondhand smoke contains the same toxic and carcinogenic chemicals inhaled by active smokers, leading to increased rates of coronary heart disease, stroke, and respiratory illnesses in exposed individuals.
